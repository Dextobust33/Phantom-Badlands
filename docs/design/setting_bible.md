# Phantom Badlands — Setting Bible

**Status:** agreed 2026-09-01 (backlog item 3). This is the reference the Dungeon Atlas, quest
text, NPC flavour, egg/companion text and the website are all written against. Keep it to a
page. If something here stops being true in the game, change this file first, then the copy.

---

## The premise, in one line

**The ground here refuses to keep its dead down, and what comes back up is drawn to the
living.**

## What a Phantom is

**A phantom is a dead thing the ground would not keep.**

They do not come up one at a time. They come up **together, in the shape of the place they
died in** — a den, a crypt, a drowned cathedral — whole enough to walk into. That is why the
locals use the same word for the place and the things inside it, and why nobody out here says
"dungeon": *a phantom came up*, the way you'd say a storm came in.

What comes up is **drawn to the living**. A phantom that is left alone starts working outward
toward the nearest holdout. That is the threat corridor, and it is the reason anyone goes down
at all: not treasure, containment.

And it wants something specific. **It wants to make you one of its own** — and given enough
trips, it does. That is the second meaning of the word, the one nobody mentions to new
arrivals: the delvers are called phantoms too, and it is not a figure of speech. Permadeath is
not bad luck and not a punishment. It is what the work does to you, on a long enough timeline,
and everyone signs on knowing the rate.

The one thing that moves cleanly between lives is what you brought back up **alive**.

*(Revised 2026-09-02 with the user. The earlier framing — "the land keeps places, and it keeps
people" — described two things happening side by side and never explained why delvers became
phantoms. This version has one cause: the dead come up, they want the living, and they get
them. Everything else follows from that.)*

## The Badlands

A frontier that lost. Not a wasteland of nothing — a wasteland of *too much*, ground so
crowded with what it won't release that nowhere stays settled.

The **trading posts are holdouts, not cities**. Each one keeps a patch of land thin and quiet
around itself; that patch is the only reliable safety there is. Walk away from a post and the
land thickens — which is exactly the post-anchored level curve the game already runs. Distance
from a post *is* danger, and everyone living there knows it in those terms.

Posts hold because they are useful to delvers and delvers are useful to them. It's a working
arrangement between people who need each other, not a heroic alliance.

## Dungeons — phantom places

Dungeons **surface**. They are not built, discovered, or abandoned: they are what a crowd of
phantoms brings up with it, the shape of the place they died in. Once one has taken root it
starts **bleeding** — its dead working outward toward the nearest holdout, drawn to whatever is
alive there. That is the threat corridor, and it is the reason anyone delves: not treasure,
containment.

Clearing one buys a real reprieve. It does not buy a permanent one. The land is still holding
whatever it was holding, and another will surface. Posts measure their safety in seasons.

A dungeon's **theme is what it kept** — a den, a crypt, a cathedral, a hatchery. The monsters
inside are not squatters; they are part of what surfaced.

## Delvers — phantom people

You are temporary and the world treats you accordingly. You are not the chosen anything. You
are one of many, you are useful, and you will be replaced.

- **Death is permanent** because that is what the things down there are for. They want the
  living, and they are patient about it.
- **Your corpse stays where you fell.** Somebody else will find it. So will you, next life.
- **The leaderboard, titles and Valor are the trace you leave** — the Badlands' way of
  remembering a name after the person is gone.

Write delvers with dignity, not tragedy. They know the terms. The tone is working people doing
dangerous work, not doomed heroes.

## Companions — what comes out alive

**Eggs come out of phantom places.** They are the one living thing a dead place produces, and
that is the point: a companion at your side is proof you went in and came out.

Companions are the setting's emotional spine because **they outlive their delvers**. When you
die, your companion doesn't. The Sanctuary keeps it, and it goes on with whoever comes next.
The bond is the only thing in the Badlands with continuous memory — you are the part that gets
replaced.

- **Variants and border tier** = how much of the place it came from it still carries.
- **Fusion** is not crafting. It is letting several become something older and less divided.
- **Breeding and the kennel** are how a line persists past any one of your lives.

## The Sanctuary — the keeping-place

The Sanctuary is yours across every life: the one address in the Badlands that stays. It holds
what survives you — companions above all. It is not a home you live in; it's the place your
things wait while you are being replaced.

## Voice — TWO registers, and knowing which one you're in

**Revised 2026-09-01 (user): go darker — anthology horror, Tales-from-the-Crypt.** The mistake
would be making *everything* camp. Anthology horror works because a grounded, straight-faced
story is framed by a theatrical host who is enjoying it. Keep both, and never mix them in the
same breath.

### 1. The world register — plain, worked, unromantic

Everything spoken by someone who *lives* here: post NPCs, merchants, quest givers, item
descriptions, tutorial and help.

- Trade towns on a bad border. No high fantasy, no prophecy, no capital-D Destiny.
- **Containment over glory.** Quests are jobs: drive it off, clear it, get them out. Nobody is
  saving the world; they are keeping this month survivable.
- The land is the antagonist and is **not** malevolent. It doesn't want anything. No dark lord.
- Delver mortality is stated **flatly**. "Most don't last two seasons." Never "tragically fallen."
- Companions are where the warmth goes. If a line has feeling in it, put it there.

### 2. The Keeper's register — ghoulish, delighted, direct

The narrator layer. Death screens, the Sanctuary greeting, Dungeon Atlas entries, load and
loading-room text, leaderboard stings, marketing copy.

**Who is talking: the Keeper of your Sanctuary.** It has been there longer than anyone, it has
met every character you have ever played, and it is *pleased* to see you again. It is not evil
and not a threat — it is an undertaker with excellent manners and a sense of humour about its
work. It keeps your companions honestly and never lies to you. It simply finds all of this
very entertaining.

This is load-bearing, not decoration: your Sanctuary is the one thing that survives your death,
so the one voice qualified to narrate a permadeath game *is* the thing that outlasts you. Each
character's life is an episode. The Keeper introduces them and sees them out.

- Direct address. It talks to *you*, not about you.
- **Gallows wit, never cruelty.** It's amused by the situation, not gloating over your loss.
- Wry understatement beats gore. "Back so soon." lands harder than a description of your wounds.
- Puns and arch phrasing are allowed here and **only** here.
- It is fond of the companions. It is fond of you, in the way one is fond of a repeat customer.
- **It never explains the mechanism** (see below). It knows more than it says, and enjoys that.

Sample register, not final copy:
> *"Ah — a new one. Come in, the last one won't be needing the chair."*
> *"Seven of you now. The wolf remembers all seven, you know. It's the only one who does."*
> *"They went in for the town's sake. That's what the paperwork says, anyway."*

### The rule for which register

If a **person in the world** is speaking → register 1. If the **game** is speaking → register 2.
A quest board is register 1. The death screen is register 2. Never let a post NPC do a pun.

**Use:** surfaced, took root, bleeding, holdout, thin ground, delver, came out alive, reprieve,
seasons, kept.
**Avoid:** ancient evil, chosen one, sealed away, the darkness, corruption, prophecy, realm.

**Never explain the mechanism.** Nobody knows *why* the land keeps things. Theories exist; none
are right. The Keeper may imply it knows. It must never say.

## What this already explains

Every line here is a mechanic the game runs today. That's the test — the fiction is a reading
of the game, not a coat of paint on it.

| Mechanic already built | Now means |
|---|---|
| Dungeons spawn on a world timer | The land keeps pushing places back up |
| Threat cones aimed at posts | A surfaced place bleeds toward the nearest holdout |
| Post-anchored monster levels | Posts keep the ground thin around them |
| Post threat cooldown after clearing | A reprieve, measured in seasons |
| Eggs / exclusive cards only from dungeons | The only things that come out alive |
| Permadeath | What is down there finally making you one of its own |
| Sanctuary survives death | The one address that stays — and the Keeper who runs it |
| Kennel companions passing between characters | The bond outlives the delver |
| Corpses left in the world | Delvers are replaced, and the land keeps the evidence |
| Leaderboard / titles / Valor | How a name is remembered after the person |
| Fusion / Tier Ascension | Letting several become something older |
| Border tier on companions | How much of its origin it still carries |

## Consequences worth building on

The conceit is generative, not just descriptive. Things that follow from it and are being
considered rather than assumed:

- **The delvers who lost become phantoms too.** If what is down there makes you one of its own,
  a dead character is not simply gone — it is now one of them. That is the fiction under
  backlog item 14 (player phantoms roaming where they fell). It is also what stops permadeath
  being purely subtractive: the dead stay in the world as something other people meet.
- **Corpses are already canon.** A character's remains stay where they fell, and other players
  can find them. That is consistent with everything here and needs no retcon.
- **The Sanctuary is the counterweight.** The phantoms take the delver; the Sanctuary keeps
  what the delver brought back up alive. Those are the two halves of the ledger, and any
  progression design should sit on one or the other.

## Deliberately undecided

- **Why** the land keeps things. Never answer this in copy (see voice rule 6).
- Whether phantom places are echoes of somewhere *real* — leave ambiguous.
- Whether anyone has ever stopped being a delver and lived. Rumour only.
- What the Keeper is, or whether it was ever a delver. It will not be drawn on this.
